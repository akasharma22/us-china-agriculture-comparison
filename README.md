# Farming Under Capitalism: A Comparative Study of Agriculture Systems in China and the United States

**ResEcon 303 Honors Colloquium · University of Massachusetts Amherst · April 2026**

**Author:** Akanksha Sharma

## Summary
A comparison of market-led agriculture in the United States and state-led agriculture in China, arguing that farmers lose bargaining power under both systems.

## Key findings
- **U.S. market concentration:** two seed companies account for 72% of planted corn acres; four meatpackers handle 85% of steer and heifer slaughter; the top four grocers' share of food sales rose from 13% (1990) to 34% (2019)
- **Seed patents:** GM seed prices rose 463% between 1990 and 2020, and farmers can no longer save seed
- **China's model:** state investment, minimum purchase prices, and platform-led rural e-commerce (including blockchain traceability), with rural incomes still at 54% of the national average
- **Where the systems meet:** WH Group's $4.7B acquisition of Smithfield Foods as a food-security strategy, and its environmental-justice costs in eastern North Carolina

## Recommendations
- **United States:** antitrust action in seeds, meatpacking, and food retail; renewed public agricultural research
- **China:** close the gap between state investment and farm incomes, and give rural communities a stake in the platforms they rely on

## Sources
USDA Economic Research Service, U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission, Iowa State CARD, *Journal of Agricultural Science*, and Xiaowei Wang's *Blockchain Chicken Farm*.
